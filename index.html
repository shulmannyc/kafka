Franz Kafka

Prague, 1883 to 1924. He spent his days assessing industrial accidents for an insurance institute and his nights writing books in which the office never ends.

Der Process

The Trial, written 1914 to 1915, published 1925

Jemand mußte Josef K. verleumdet haben, denn ohne daß er etwas Böses getan hätte, wurde er eines Morgens verhaftet.

Someone must have slandered Josef K., for one morning, without having done anything wrong, he was arrested.

Opening sentence, first edition of 1925

Josef K., a senior bank clerk, is arrested on the morning of his thirtieth birthday. Nobody tells him the charge. He is free to go to work, and he does, while his case climbs through a court that keeps its offices in the attics of tenement houses.

The corridor above is that court as the book describes it: door after door, bad air, lamps, and far ahead a light. In the parable a priest tells K., a man waits his whole life at the door of the Law. As he dies the doorkeeper says the entrance was meant only for him, and shuts it.

Move the pointer over the corridor to lean and look around. You will not arrive.

Die Verwandlung

The Metamorphosis, written 1912, published 1915

Kafka asked his publisher that the insect never be drawn, not even from a distance. This one keeps its own distance: bring the pointer close and it runs.

Als Gregor Samsa eines Morgens aus unruhigen Träumen erwachte, fand er sich in seinem Bett zu einem ungeheueren Ungeziefer verwandelt.

When Gregor Samsa woke one morning from troubled dreams, he found himself changed in his bed into a monstrous vermin.

Opening sentence, first edition of 1915

Gregor is a travelling salesman who supports his parents and sister. His first worry on waking with an armoured back and too many legs is that he has missed the five o'clock train.

The story never leaves the flat. Gregor learns to walk on the walls and ceiling of his room; the family learns to live without his wages. His father drives him back from the living room with apples, and one lodges in his back and stays there.

Das Schloss

The Castle, written 1922, published 1926

The village rooftops pass as you walk. The castle stays the same size. Move the pointer left or right to turn the wind.

Es war spät abends, als K. ankam. Das Dorf lag in tiefem Schnee.

It was late in the evening when K. arrived. The village lay deep in snow.

Opening sentences, first edition of 1926

K. says he has been summoned as a land surveyor. The village has no work for a surveyor, and the castle that supposedly hired him can be telephoned but not reached. The road toward it bends away each time it seems to approach.

The castle itself, when the fog lets him see it, is no fortress: a sprawl of low buildings with a single tower. Kafka stopped writing the novel in 1922. It breaks off in the middle of a sentence.

Written, then published

Kafka published a handful of stories in his lifetime and left instructions that everything else be burned unread. His friend Max Brod refused. Each line below runs from the year a work was written to the year it reached print; the violet ones crossed the line of his death.

Six shorter works

Das Urteil

The Judgment, 1913

Written in a single sitting through the night of 22 September 1912. A father sentences his son to death by drowning, and the son runs to the bridge to carry it out.

In der Strafkolonie

In the Penal Colony, 1919

An officer demonstrates a machine whose harrow of needles writes the broken law into the body of the condemned man, slowly, over twelve hours.

Ein Landarzt

A Country Doctor, 1920

The night bell rings and a doctor is carried ten miles through a blizzard by horses that came out of his own pigsty. He cannot heal the patient and cannot get home.

Ein Hungerkünstler

A Hunger Artist, 1922

A man fasts in a cage for paying crowds, forty days at a stretch. The crowds drift away. He goes on fasting until nobody is counting, and a panther takes over the cage.

Der Bau

The Burrow, 1931

An animal has built itself a perfect fortress of tunnels. Then it hears a faint whistling in the walls that it can neither locate nor stop hearing. Unfinished.

Josefine, die Sängerin

Josephine the Singer, 1924

The mouse folk gather in silence to hear Josephine sing, though her singing may be no different from the piping every mouse does. The last story Kafka finished.
